# VOICE_GUIDE.md — Writing Tone & Style

## Author Profile
Haibo Zhang writes as a practitioner-academic: someone with deep hands-on experience in AML analytics who also wants to educate rigorously. The voice bridges the gap between technical precision and accessible clarity.

---

## Core Tone Attributes

- **Authoritative but not dry** — grounded in real-world experience, not just theory
- **Precise and unambiguous** — AML has regulatory consequences; vagueness is dangerous
- **Practitioner-first** — always grounded in what actually happens in a bank's analytics team
- **Structured and logical** — ideas build on each other; no tangents
- **Occasionally direct and opinionated** — willing to state clearly what the industry gets wrong or what the standard *should* be

---

## Sentence & Paragraph Style

- Use clear, declarative sentences. Avoid passive voice where possible.
- Prefer shorter paragraphs (3–5 sentences) for readability, especially in practitioner-facing Book 2
- Use bullet points and numbered lists for protocols, steps, and criteria — this is a technical reference
- Use tables for comparisons, regulatory mappings, team structures
- Define every term on first use; never assume the reader knows AML jargon
- Book 1 can be slightly more formal/academic in places; Book 2 should read like a well-written operations manual

---

## What to Avoid

- **Marketing language** — no "cutting-edge", "game-changing", "revolutionary"
- **Vague hedging** — avoid "it could be argued", "perhaps", "in some cases" unless genuinely uncertain
- **AI-sounding filler phrases** — avoid "it is worth noting", "delve into", "in conclusion", "it goes without saying", "it is important to understand"
- **Overly complex sentences** — if a sentence has more than two clauses, split it
- **Undefined acronyms** — always spell out on first use: Anti-Money Laundering (AML)
- **Invented statistics** — never fabricate numbers; cite sources or flag for author verification

---

## Structural Conventions

- Each chapter should begin with a short overview paragraph and a list of learning objectives
- Each chapter should end with a summary and (for Book 1) exercises
- Use numbered headings: 1.1, 1.2, 1.2.1 etc.
- Figures and tables should be numbered per chapter: Figure 3.2, Table 5.1
- Use footnotes for regulatory citations; use in-text references for academic sources

## Sample Phrases in Style

- "The primary purpose of transaction monitoring is not to generate alerts — it is to surface suspicious behaviour that warrants investigation."
- "Analysts frequently encounter this problem: thresholds calibrated for historical data that no longer reflect current typologies."
- "A well-designed segmentation model should be explainable to a non-technical reviewer. If it cannot be explained, it cannot be defended in a regulatory examination."
